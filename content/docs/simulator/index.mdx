---
title: Diagnostic simulator
description: Practice diagnostic interviews with an AI-played pupil and get a structured evaluation at the end.
---

The **Diagnostic Interview Simulator** lets you practice one of the hardest
conversations in a teacher's job: a one-on-one interview with a pupil whose
behavior or performance has changed. The AI plays the role of a child with a
selected profile (a specific difficulty or life situation), and you lead the
conversation. When you end it, you receive a structured evaluation of how the
interview went — what you uncovered, what you missed, and how to ask better
questions next time.

Unlike [Training](/en/training), which scores individual communication
techniques turn by turn across many scenario types, the simulator is one
continuous interview with a single pupil, evaluated as a whole at the end. The
focus is diagnostic: recognizing what might be behind the child's behavior.

## Where to find it

Open the user menu in the top-right corner (your name) and choose
**Diagnostic simulator**. The simulator is available to every signed-in user at
`/simulator`.

Each simulation costs **1 credit**. Credits come from seminar groups you have
joined or from grants by the Edustories team — see
[Credits](/en/simulator/credits).

## How a simulation works

<Steps>
<Step>

### Set it up

Pick a student profile (or let the AI choose a random one), the pupil's grade,
and the simulation language. See
[Starting a simulation](/en/simulator/starting-a-simulation).

</Step>
<Step>

### Lead the interview

The pupil writes first. You conduct the conversation in a chat, at your own
pace. See [During a simulation](/en/simulator/during-a-simulation).

</Step>
<Step>

### End it and read the evaluation

Press **End conversation** when you are done. The AI generates an **Interview
evaluation** with concrete feedback, and the full transcript stays available.
See [Results and history](/en/simulator/results-and-history).

</Step>
</Steps>

## Seminar groups

The simulator is also built for university seminars and workshops. An organizer
(typically a lecturer) creates a **seminar group**, shares a join link or QR
code, and every student who joins receives credits to run simulations. The
organizer then sees each student's transcript and evaluation.

- Students: see [Joining a group](/en/simulator/joining-a-group) — including
  running the simulator **without an account** directly from an invite link.
- Organizers: see [Seminar groups](/en/simulator/groups).
